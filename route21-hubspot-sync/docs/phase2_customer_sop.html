Phase 2 – Customer Invoice Analytics | Sales & Customer Service SOP

Audience: Sales Representatives & Customer Service Workflow: Phase 2 – Customer Invoice Analytics Last Updated: June 2026

Purpose

This workflow analyzes Business Central (BC) sales invoice history and writes customer health metrics into HubSpot. It tells the sales team how recently a customer has ordered, how much they typically spend per order, how often they order, and whether their ordering behavior has changed. These fields automatically update each time the workflow runs.

This workflow does not modify BC, create HubSpot companies, or delete records.

Manual Only Launched on demand by an analyst in n8n. It is not scheduled to run automatically.
Note for Sales Reps: The metrics in HubSpot reflect the last time the workflow was run. If you need the most current data, ask an analyst to run Phase 2.

Step-by-Step Flow

Stage 1 — Setup (Once Per Run)
1
Load Credentials & Get BC Token The workflow loads API credentials and requests a secure access token from Microsoft Azure to connect to Business Central. This token is refreshed automatically throughout the run.
2
Fetch All HubSpot Companies Retrieves all HubSpot companies tagged as Customers under Engelman's Bakery with an ERP account number. Paginated in groups of 200. The current Ordering Status and First Invoice Date are also fetched here so the workflow knows what's already set before writing.
Stage 2 — Batch Processing (Repeats for Every 10 Companies)
3
Build Invoice Filter Groups the current 10 companies into a BC filter string by their ERP account numbers (e.g., customerNumber eq '12345' or customerNumber eq '67890').
4
Fetch Last 2,000 Invoices for Batch Source A Calls BC's Sales Invoices API for the current 10 customers, ordered most-recent-first. Up to 2,000 invoices are returned across the batch. This data drives ordering status, last invoice date, average order size, and average days between orders.
5
Fetch YTD Invoices Source B Calls BC again for the same 10 customers, but filtered to only invoices with a posting date on or after January 1st of the current year. All matching invoices are returned with no cap — used exclusively to calculate Actual Sales YTD.
6
Fetch Closed Won Deals Source C Searches HubSpot for any Closed Won deal where the ERP account number matches one of the current 10 customers and the deal close date is on or after January 1, 2026. Used only to set or reset First Invoice Date.
7
Calculate Metrics Runs all calculations for the 10 companies using the data from Steps 4–6. Determines ordering status, last invoice date, average order size, average days between orders, YTD sales, and whether First Invoice Date needs to be written. Only companies with changes are queued for update.
8
Update HubSpot Sends calculated values to HubSpot in a single batch API call for all companies that need updating. Companies with no changes are skipped.
9
Repeat for Next 10 Companies Steps 3–8 repeat until all HubSpot companies have been processed.

Ordering Status — What Each Status Means

The Ordering Status field tells you where a customer currently stands relative to their own historical ordering pattern. It is based entirely on their last 10 invoices from BC and is recalculated every time the workflow runs.

Actively Ordering

The customer's most recent invoice is recent enough that there is no cause for concern. They are ordering within their normal frequency window.

→ No action needed. Continue regular account management.

Outside Ordering Pattern

The customer has not placed an order in longer than twice their average ordering frequency. For example, if they normally order every 10 days, this triggers when they have gone more than 20 days without an order.

This is the early warning signal — the customer has gone quiet but is not yet considered lost.

→ Reach out proactively. A check-in call or email at this stage is often enough to re-engage.

Potentially Lost

The customer has not placed an order in longer than three times their average ordering frequency. Using the same example, this triggers after 30+ days with no order from a customer who typically orders every 10 days.

This does not mean the customer has left — it means they are significantly outside their normal pattern and need direct attention.

→ Escalate to the assigned salesperson. Prioritize personal outreach.

Not Ordered

No invoices were found in Business Central for this account. This typically applies to newly onboarded customers who have not yet placed their first order, or accounts set up in HubSpot before their first BC transaction.

→ Confirm the account is fully onboarded. Follow up with the salesperson to initiate first delivery.

How Ordering Status Is Calculated

The workflow uses only the customer's last 10 invoices from BC to determine their ordering pattern. Here is the exact logic, step by step:

Step 1 — Average Days Between Orders

The 10 most recent invoices are sorted oldest to newest. The gap in days between each consecutive pair is measured, and those gaps are averaged.

Formula:
Avg Days = (gap1 + gap2 + … + gap9) ÷ 9
Requires at least 2 invoices. If only 1 invoice exists, default thresholds of 14 / 28 days are used instead.
Example: A customer's last 3 invoice dates are Jan 1, Jan 12, and Jan 25.
Gap 1: 11 days  |  Gap 2: 13 days
Avg Days Between Orders = (11 + 13) ÷ 2 = 12 days

Step 2 — Risk Day and Lost Day

Using the average, the workflow calculates two thresholds from the date of the last invoice:

Risk Day = Last Invoice Date + (Avg Days × 2)   [minimum 5 days]

Lost Day = Last Invoice Date + (Avg Days × 3)   [minimum 7 days]
Continuing the example: Last invoice was Jan 25. Avg = 12 days.
Risk Day = Jan 25 + 24 days = Feb 18
Lost Day = Jan 25 + 36 days = Mar 2

Step 3 — Assign Status Based on Today's Date

If today < Risk Day → Actively Ordering

If today ≥ Risk Day but < Lost Day → Outside Ordering Pattern

If today ≥ Lost Day → Potentially Lost

If no invoices in BC → Not Ordered
Important: A customer who orders irregularly (e.g., monthly) will have a wider window before triggering Outside Ordering Pattern than a customer who orders weekly. The thresholds are personalized to each account's own behavior — not a fixed number of days for everyone.

How Average Order Size Is Calculated

Average Order Size is the average dollar value of the last 10 invoices for the customer. It uses the total amount including tax from each invoice.

Avg Order Size = Sum of last 10 invoice totals ÷ 10
Rounded to the nearest cent. If fewer than 10 invoices exist, all available invoices are used.
Example: Last 4 invoices: $420, $380, $510, $390
Avg Order Size = ($420 + $380 + $510 + $390) ÷ 4 = $425.00
What this tells you: If a customer's recent invoice amounts are significantly lower than their average, it may indicate they are reducing their order volume — a useful early signal even before ordering frequency changes.

How Average Days Between Orders Is Calculated

This field measures how many days typically pass between this customer's orders, based on their last 10 invoices. It drives the risk and lost day thresholds described above.

Sort last 10 invoices oldest → newest

Measure gap in days between each consecutive pair

Average all gaps → round to 1 decimal place
Requires at least 2 invoices. If only 1 invoice exists, this field is left blank and default thresholds of 14 days (risk) / 28 days (lost) are used for ordering status.
Example — Weekly customer: Invoices every ~7 days
Avg Days Between Orders ≈ 7  |  Risk threshold at 14 days  |  Lost threshold at 21 days

Example — Monthly customer: Invoices every ~30 days
Avg Days Between Orders ≈ 30  |  Risk threshold at 60 days  |  Lost threshold at 90 days

Actual Sales YTD

This field shows the total dollar amount invoiced to the customer so far this calendar year, from January 1st through the most recent invoice in BC at the time the workflow ran.

YTD Sales = Sum of all invoice totals where posting date ≥ Jan 1 of current year
No cap on number of invoices — all YTD invoices are included. Rounded to the nearest cent. Tax is included in the total (matches BC invoice totals).
Note: This resets automatically each January 1st when the workflow next runs — no manual action is required to start the new year.

Last Invoice Date

The posting date of the most recent invoice posted in BC for this customer. Updated every time the workflow runs if a newer invoice exists.

First Invoice Date — How It Works

First Invoice Date records when the customer placed their first order under the current relationship. It is used to understand customer tenure and to track re-onboarded accounts.

Historical Accounts

For existing customers, First Invoice Date was set through a one-time manual import by the analyst team. The workflow will not overwrite these historical values unless a qualifying new deal is won (see below).

New Customers (Won on or After January 1, 2026)

When a deal in HubSpot is marked Closed Won with a close date on or after January 1, 2026, the workflow will look for the first BC invoice posted on or after that deal's close date and write it as the First Invoice Date.

Trigger: A Closed Won deal exists with close date ≥ Jan 1, 2026

AND the deal's close date is more recent than the current First Invoice Date (or the field is blank)

First Invoice Date = Date of first BC invoice on or after the deal's close date

Re-Onboarding Accounts

If a customer who already has a First Invoice Date wins a new Closed Won deal with a close date after the existing First Invoice Date, the field is reset. This captures the start of the new relationship rather than the original one.

What triggers an update:
  • A Closed Won deal is logged in HubSpot with close date ≥ Jan 1, 2026
  • The deal's ERP account number matches the HubSpot company
  • The deal close date is newer than the current First Invoice Date (or the field is blank)
What does NOT trigger an update:
  • A deal won before January 1, 2026
  • A deal that is not in "Closed Won" stage
  • The field already having a value with no newer qualifying deal

Common Questions

A customer just placed a big order but still shows "Potentially Lost" — why?

The workflow only reflects data as of the last time it was run. If a new invoice was posted in BC after the last run, HubSpot has not yet been updated. Ask an analyst to re-run Phase 2 to refresh the data.

A customer's First Invoice Date is wrong — how do I fix it?

If the date shown is incorrect and no new deal has been won, the value can be corrected directly in HubSpot by editing the property on the company record. The workflow will not overwrite a manually set value unless a qualifying new Closed Won deal (close date ≥ Jan 1, 2026) is logged.

Why does a very infrequent customer take longer to show "Potentially Lost"?

Because the threshold is based on each customer's own ordering pattern. A customer who orders every 60 days won't trigger "Outside Ordering Pattern" until 120 days have passed — whereas a weekly customer triggers it after only 14 days. This prevents false alarms on seasonal or low-frequency accounts.

What if a customer has fewer than 10 invoices?

All available invoices are used. The average order size and average days between orders are calculated from however many invoices exist. If only 1 invoice exists, the ordering status thresholds default to 14 days (Outside Ordering Pattern) and 28 days (Potentially Lost).

Does this workflow update billing or address information?

No. Billing address, credit limit, payment terms, and salesperson assignment are managed by Phase 1 – BC → HubSpot Companies Final. Phase 2 only writes the invoice analytics fields listed in the table above.
